# How Indian safari and birding sites look, and how We Rove can beat them

Study date: 1 November 2026. Seven sites were read (text and layout) and screenshotted (homepage, desktop, above the fold). Three could not be read: **Pugdundee Safaris** and **Taj Safaris** block automated visitors, and **WildTrails** has a broken redirect, so they are not covered.

Prices, ratings and review counts below are quoted as each site shows them, and I haven't verified them independently. Colours and layout come from the screenshots, not from the sites' code.

## 2. What they look like

**Almost all of them look the same.** The common recipe:

1. A full-width photo of a tiger, dark overlay, white headline, one orange or green button.
2. A white navigation bar with dropdown menus (5–9 items).
3. Cards with a photo, a title and a "View tour" button.
4. A strip of review logos (TripAdvisor, Google, Trustpilot).
5. A pop-up, cookie banner or chat widget on top of all of it.

| Site | First impression from the screenshot |
|---|---|
| Nature Safari India | Full-bleed tiger photo, dark overlay, orange buttons, a trust strip sitting right under the hero ("TripAdvisor 5.0 · Trustpilot 4.8 · Google 4.9 · #1 of 155 Tours in India"). Clean, confident, **generic luxury safari**. |
| Big Cats India | Dark tiger photo. A cookie banner **covers the headline** on first load, and the heading underneath is washed-out grey. Floating WhatsApp button. |
| Treks and Trails | A sliding banner with a trip title in a white box, and a **WhatsApp widget with a ticking countdown clock** covering the hero. Looks like a booking marketplace. |
| Foliage Outdoors | Forest-road photo with white menu text placed straight on top of the image, so the menu is hard to read. Slider arrows and a green "Chat with us" bubble. |
| Bubo Birding | Atmospheric flock-of-birds hero, but the white menu text sits on a bright sky and is **barely legible**. A hot-pink button. The most "birding-feeling" site, though. |
| Jungle Lodges | A modal pop-up about a phone ban appears over the page before you can read anything. |
| Sanctuary | Magazine style: big editorial photos, thin lines, deep green. The best-looking of the set, but it sells subscriptions, not trips. |

### Patterns across the set

- **Heroes are always photos of big cats.** None show an illustration, hand-drawn art, or a designed identity.
- **Orange, forest green and black dominate.** Nothing looks different from the next site.
- **Interruptions are everywhere.** Four of the seven sites put something (cookie banner, pop-up, chat widget, countdown) over the first screen.
- **Overlaid menus are a legibility problem** on the two sites that put white text directly on busy photos.
- **Homepages are very long.** Nature Safari India's runs to about 60,000 characters of text, and Treks and Trails shows about 60 trip cards. They repeat the same trips and calls to action many times.

## 3. How they present their content

### Hero and navigation
- One headline claiming expertise ("Luxury Tiger Safari Specialists in India Since 2000", "Most Trusted Wildlife Tour Operator in Central India") and two buttons.
- Menus built around **products** (Tiger Safari Tours, Luxury, Photographic, Fixed Departures) or **parks** (Park Guides by region).
- Contact details sit in the header: phone, email, an "Enquire" or "Book Now" button.

### Trip and destination pages
- Same shape everywhere: photo gallery → overview → **day-by-day itinerary** → inclusions and exclusions → FAQ → enquiry form.
- **Sticky tabs** (Overview / Itinerary / Inclusions / Dates) on Foliage and Treks and Trails make long pages easy to scan.
- Big Cats puts a stats row at the top of each trip (14 nights, 4 reserves, 23 jeep safaris, 5 fixed departures). It scans very fast.
- Bubo's richest content is **trip reports**: a diary of each day, species photo captions, linked eBird checklists, and honest notes on what they missed. It builds trust better than any marketing copy.

### Prices and dates
- **Premium operators hide prices** (Nature Safari India, Big Cats India): they send you to a quote form and promise a reply, e.g. "within 12 hours" or "24 hours".
- **Budget operators show them**: Treks and Trails lists a 3-night Sattal–Pangot birding trip at ₹18,500 and a 2-night Pench safari at ₹21,000. Foliage lists its Sattal trip at ₹38,900–40,900.
- Fixed departures with dates and a "spots left" badge are used by Nature Safari India. Big Cats shows named seasonal windows (Early Winter, Christmas Special, New Year).

### Trust
- Nature Safari India does it best: review counts from three platforms, a founder with "38 years in the field", named naturalists labelled "naturalist since 2006", and a promise that a senior naturalist reads every enquiry.
- Big Cats shows Google, TripAdvisor and Trustpilot scores and a clear cancellation table.
- Several sites claim "years in business" inconsistently (Nature Safari India says "over 2 decades", "26 years" and "25 years" in different places).

### Contact
- Enquiry forms are the main route. Nature Safari India's form has a 200-country dropdown and asks for travel dates, duration and group size.
- WhatsApp is usually just a link or floating button. Nobody builds the form around it.
- Several sites offer 3–4 contact paths at once (two phones, two emails, a form, WhatsApp, chat).

## 4. What they get wrong

1. **They all look alike.** A visitor comparing three sites sees the same tiger, the same orange button.
2. **Things block the first screen:** cookie banners, countdown widgets, modals.
3. **Quote-form-only booking** at the premium end, even though customers want to talk to someone.
4. **Sloppy details, which cost trust:** typos ("expeirence", "Choise", "Birdiwatching"), "Enter a tagline for your collection" showing live on Treks and Trails, two different prices on the same Foliage page, a Jungle Lodges offer still dated 2020, broken "#" links.
5. **Birding is an afterthought** at most of them: Foliage's Sattal trip isn't on its homepage, and Big Cats' birding is one menu item.
6. **Thin or generic beginner help.** Few explain how a first-time birder will actually spend a day.
7. **Heavy, repetitive pages** with the same trips shown four or five times.

## 5. Where We Rove can win, and where it can't yet

### Where our design can be clearly better
| Their weakness | How the mockups answer it |
|---|---|
| Every site looks the same | Themes G, H, I and J use hand-drawn line art, arches, tracks, fireflies and block-print bands. No competitor has anything like it, so people remember it. |
| Interruptions on the first screen | Nothing covers our hero. No cookie wall, countdown or pop-up. |
| Hard-to-read menus over photos | Our menus sit on solid backgrounds. |
| Form-only booking | A phone number and a prefilled-WhatsApp button come first on every page. The form simply opens WhatsApp. |
| Copy and detail errors | One shared content file (`shared.js`) holds every fact once, so a number can't appear differently in two places. |
| Birding as an afterthought | Birding has its own section, and the "How we travel" rules (no playback, no baiting) speak directly to serious birders. |
| Long, repetitive homepages | Each section appears once. |
| Slow, heavy pages | Static pages, compressed images (the bird photos shrank from 29 MB to about 1.4 MB). |

Two things none of the homepages I read showed: a **when-to-go season calendar** and a **"a day on safari" timeline**. Both are in our mockups (H, I, J and the A–F themes).

### Where they are ahead of us today
This is the honest part. The mockups can't yet match these:

1. **Real photography.** Every competitor leads with excellent tiger and bird photos. Ours are placeholders for the safari destinations. The design can't carry the site alone, so photos are the single biggest need.
2. **Reviews and ratings.** Nature Safari India shows hundreds. We show none yet. Start collecting on Google Business Profile now.
3. **Named people.** Their founders and naturalists are named with years of experience. We're keeping the team anonymous, so we need other proof (see below).
4. **Prices and dates.** Budget competitors show real prices and departures. We show none yet.

### How to build trust while staying anonymous
- Show **credentials without names**: "Basic and Advanced Mountaineering, Search and Rescue trained".
- A **response promise** next to the contact buttons ("we reply the same day").
- **Honest trip reports** in Bubo's style, including what you missed. This is the cheapest trust-builder and needs no names or faces.
- A **clear cancellation table** (Big Cats and Treks and Trails both have one), plus a "from ₹" price per destination.
- **Specific inclusions**, e.g. "5 jeep safaris with permits" rather than "safaris".
- Real **Google reviews**, pulled in with the reviewer's own name.

## 7. More themes (now built)

These came directly from the gaps above, and all three now exist as mockups: [K](k-fieldguide.html), [L](l-cartographer.html) and [M](m-linocut.html).

### K · Field Guide (best for the birding side)
Pages laid out like a **classic bird field guide**: numbered "plates" per destination, each with a species checklist and tick-boxes, a "birds you could see" panel, and species names in small caps. It beats Bubo's plain layout and gives serious birders a reason to trust us. It's also strong for search, since species names are what birders look up. Works equally well with illustrations before real photos arrive.

### L · Cartographer (best for "no fixed list of locations")
A hand-drawn **map of India** with pins for the places people ask about most, and an open "anywhere else" corner. Click a pin to see the park. It solves the "don't state a number of locations" problem naturally, because a map never implies a finite list. The topographic contour style from our placeholders fits it.

### M · Linocut (best for no photos, strongest brand)
Bold two-colour **linocut-style prints**. In the mockup the existing photos are converted to prints by a script, and the same treatment will work on the real photos when they arrive. It looks like nothing else in the category and doesn't depend on photos, so it can launch before the photo shoot. Print style also works on itineraries, stickers and jeep banners.

## 9. Recommended next steps

1. Pick a direction: **H or J** for distinctiveness, or ask to see K, L or M first.
2. **Photos first.** 2–4 real photos per destination, plus a couple of team-in-the-field shots with no faces.
3. Start collecting **Google reviews** from past travellers.
4. Write **one honest trip report** (a recent Sattal or Corbett trip). It becomes the model for the blog and a trust-builder in its own right.
5. Decide on **"from ₹" prices** and a **cancellation table**.
