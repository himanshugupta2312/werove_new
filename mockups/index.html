Art layer: birds and growing vines

Line-art birds drift slowly across the screen in small flocks, and vines grow up the side margins as you scroll. It's built into G and can be switched on for any other theme by adding ?ambient to its address. Compare: A · B · C · D · E · F.

Rules it follows: art stays in the margins and hero, never behind body text. Movement is slow. The vines hide on phones. Visitors whose device is set to reduce motion get still drawings instead.

Latest changes

  • Safari first, everywhere. Safari destinations list first, and the boho themes give safari the larger space, with birding as a smaller "and for the birders" section.
  • No fixed number of locations. Lines like "6 destinations" and "six places we know" are gone. Destinations are framed as the places travellers ask about most, followed by "If it's wild and in India, ask us."
  • New art effects in ambient.js: pugmark trails (H), fireflies (I) and block-print stamps (J), alongside the birds and vines.
  • Contact: +91 96543 43996 for calls and WhatsApp, and werove.in@gmail.com. The address is gone from every theme.
  • Booking happens on calls and WhatsApp. Every theme puts Call and WhatsApp buttons up front. The enquiry form doesn't send email; it opens WhatsApp with the visitor's details filled in, so it needs no backend and every lead lands where you already reply.
  • Anonymous team: "Who guides you" lists roles and credentials (naturalist and mountaineer, lead birder, trip planner) with no names or faces.
  • Destination photos: contour-map placeholders in img/tadoba.webp, img/pench.webp and so on. Drop a real photo in with the same filename and every theme picks it up.
  • Field Notes (blog) is built but hidden. Add ?blog to any theme's URL to preview it, e.g. f-canopy.html?blog. When you have 3 or more posts, flip showBlog to true in shared.js.
  • Conservation tab: dropped. Its useful parts live on as the How we travel section.

Where content lives

shared.js holds the phone, email, destinations, seasons, ethics rules, crew roles, FAQ and blog posts. Themes D, E and F read everything from it. Themes A, B and C still have their destination text written into the page, but use it for contact details, crew and the blog. The real site would use this same single-source setup.
